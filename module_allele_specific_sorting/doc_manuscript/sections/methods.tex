\subsection{Rationale}

A locus can be strongly differentiated among populations whenever the populations differ in allele frequency at that locus. Linkage disequilibrium among populations, in contrast, arises only when two loci differ among the \emph{same} populations in a consistent direction. High \FST\ at many loci therefore implies high multilocus LD only if the loci share their population partition. If ancestry has sorted independently at different loci --- different loci fixing different parental alleles in different subsets of populations --- strong differentiation can accumulate across the genome while LD remains close to what recombination alone would leave. We refer to this as allele-specific (or heterogeneous, locus-specific) sorting.

Epistatic selection makes a different prediction. The strength of selection against an incompatible combination of parental alleles does not decline with physical distance between the interacting loci. Sorting driven by BDMIs is therefore expected to generate associations between specific, often unlinked, loci rather than a general elevation of LD at short distances \citep{orr1995population,schumer2014high}. Because such partners would be a small subset of all pairs of unlinked loci, they need to be sought in the tails of the distribution of long-range associations rather than in its mean.

\subsection{Units, populations and orientation}

All analyses used the 20,807 LD-reduced units of the DI25 panel (markers with parental diagnostic index $>-25$; from-scratch hybrids-only clustering with a 5~cM merge cap and the decay-relative Stage-2 quality gate $\rho=0.5$), represented by their best SNP (clusters of more than two markers) or representative SNP, using strictly observed genotypes. This is the same unit set as the population-partitioning analyses. Genotypes of the 165 hybrid individuals in 20 populations were oriented so that the allele dosage counts the \Fa\ allele, as identified from the parental reference samples. For each unit $i$ and population $k$ we computed the \Fa-allele frequency $F_{ik}$; its among-population profile $\mathbf{F}_i$ is the basis for the among-population statistics below. Per-unit differentiation was estimated with the \citet{weir1984estimating} \FST\ among the 20 hybrid populations. Directional sorting was classified with the locked convention used throughout the project (near-fixation at $\phi=0.85$, sorting threshold $\tau=0.6$, binomial test of direction at $\alpha=0.05$), giving 1,215 units sorted toward \Fa\ and 365 toward \Fp.

\subsection{Three pairwise statistics}

\paragraph{Among-population concordance.} For units $i$ and $j$, concordance $c_{ij}$ is the Pearson correlation of their oriented population profiles across the 20 populations. Because profiles are oriented, the sign is interpretable: a positive value means that the populations enriched for \Fa\ ancestry at one locus are also enriched at the other. We also computed concordance after residualising each profile on a leave-one-chromosome-out estimate of each population's genome-wide ancestry, which removes the shared ancestry gradient among populations.

\paragraph{Among-population LD and its \FST\ ceiling.} Following the decomposition of LD in subdivided populations by \citet{ohta1982linkage}, the among-population component of LD between two loci, normalised as a correlation, is
\begin{equation}
r_{ST,ij}=\frac{\operatorname{cov}_k(F_{ik},F_{jk})}{\sqrt{\bar F_i(1-\bar F_i)\,\bar F_j(1-\bar F_j)}}=c_{ij}\sqrt{G_iG_j},
\qquad G_i=\frac{\operatorname{var}_k(F_{ik})}{\bar F_i(1-\bar F_i)},
\label{eq:rst}
\end{equation}
where $G_i$ is the (Nei-type) among-population differentiation of locus $i$ and means and variances are taken over the 20 populations with equal weight. Equation~\eqref{eq:rst} separates the two ingredients of among-population LD. The product $G_iG_j$ is a \emph{ceiling}: the value $r_{ST,ij}^2$ would take if the two loci partitioned the populations identically ($|c_{ij}|=1$). It depends only on the marginal differentiation of each locus. The \emph{realised fraction} of that ceiling is $c_{ij}^2$. We summarise it over a set of pairs as $\sum r_{ST}^2/\sum G_iG_j$. For two unrelated 20-population profiles the expected $c^2$ is $1/19\approx0.053$, which is therefore the chance level of the realised fraction.

\paragraph{Within-population LD.} Within-population LD $r_w$ is the correlation between oriented genotypes after centring each unit within each population, pooled over all individuals (i.e.\ a sample-size-weighted within-population correlation). A second version, $r_w^{\mathrm{adj}}$, additionally regresses each unit, within populations, on each individual's leave-one-chromosome-out hybrid index; this removes admixture LD generated by variation in overall ancestry among individuals of the same population. Missing genotypes (2.7\%) were set to the population mean. The pooled $r_w$ is diluted for any pair in which one locus is monomorphic in a population: that population contributes nothing to the covariance but still contributes the other locus's variance to the denominator. Because sorted units are, by definition, near-fixed in most populations, this dilution affects them disproportionately. For the comparison of sorted and unsorted units we therefore also computed $r_w^{\mathrm{poly}}$, the pooled within-population correlation restricted, for each pair, to the populations in which both loci segregate.

\subsection{Distance decay and chromosome-block bootstrap}

All 9.6 million within-chromosome unit pairs and all 206.8 million cross-chromosome pairs were evaluated. Pairs were binned by physical distance (0--5~kb, 5--20~kb, 20--100~kb, 100--500~kb, 0.5--2~Mb, 2--10~Mb, $>10$~Mb) and, as a sensitivity analysis, by genetic distance using the per-chromosome recombination map; cross-chromosome pairs formed an additional ``unlinked'' category. Pairs were also stratified by the smaller of the two units' \FST\ (tertiles of unit \FST). Uncertainty was estimated with a chromosome-block bootstrap (2,000 replicates) that resampled the 26 chromosomes with replacement. Within-chromosome pairs were weighted by the number of times their chromosome was drawn; cross-chromosome pairs between chromosomes $a$ and $b$ were weighted by the product of the two draw counts, so that pairs between two copies of the same chromosome were never treated as unlinked. Treating millions of non-independent pairs as independent would grossly understate uncertainty.

\subsection{Sorted units versus matched unsorted units}

If sorting acted on haplotypes --- for example by selective sweeps or by retention of long ancestry blocks around selected loci --- the neighbours of sorted units should share their population partition and their LD over larger distances than expected. We tested this by comparing the neighbourhoods (all units within 2~Mb) of 1,577 of the 1,580 directionally sorted units (anchors; three lie outside the recombination map) with those of unsorted units matched to them. For each anchor, the five nearest unsorted units were drawn, with replacement, on standardised \FST, $\log_{10}$ local recombination rate, parental allele-frequency difference and local unit density (units within $\pm100$~kb, which determines how many close neighbours a unit can have). Matching on \FST\ is essential because concordance increases with \FST\ genome-wide; without it, any difference could reflect differentiation rather than sorting. For each focal unit we averaged concordance, residualised concordance and the within-population LD statistics over its neighbours in each distance bin, and compared anchors with controls (controls weighted by how often they were drawn) with the chromosome-block bootstrap of the difference. Anchors sorted toward \Fa\ and \Fp\ were also analysed separately.

\subsection{Long-range associations and a chromosome-wise permutation null}

For all cross-chromosome pairs we computed three statistics: residualised among-population concordance (BDMI partners resolved into compatible combinations should distinguish the same populations), raw concordance (included as a contrast: it also reflects the shared genome-wide ancestry gradient), and within-population LD $r_w^{\mathrm{adj}}$ (conspecific-ancestry association within populations while loci still segregate). Under the orientation used, positive values correspond to same-ancestry combinations, the combinations favoured if incompatibilities act between heterospecific alleles.

The null distribution was generated by a chromosome-wise permutation. For each chromosome independently, population labels (among-population statistics) or individuals within populations (within-population statistic) were permuted, jointly for all units on that chromosome. This keeps all within-chromosome structure and every unit's marginal distribution, and removes only associations between chromosomes. We used 50 permutations. For a grid of thresholds we counted observed and null pairs in the positive ($r\ge T$) and negative ($r\le -T$) tails. Because shared population structure (among populations) and relatedness (within populations) inflate both tails relative to this null, tail counts alone are not evidence of epistasis. Epistasis between compatible ancestries instead predicts an excess of positive over negative associations, so the primary test statistic was the tail asymmetry $(n_+-n_-)/(n_++n_-)$, compared with its distribution across permutations. Primary thresholds were $T^*=0.8$ for the among-population statistics and $T^*=0.35$ for within-population LD. For pairs beyond $T^*$ we recorded the weakest leave-one-population-out correlation, to identify associations driven by a single population, and, for each unit, the number of tail partners, to identify hubs. Finally, we compared the fraction of tail-pair endpoints falling in the colleague's BDMI candidate regions (cutoff index 13) and among sorted units with the corresponding null fractions. The BDMI regions are nodes without partner information, so only endpoint enrichment, not edge identity, can be tested.

\subsection{Relation to the neutral simulations}

The comparisons above are internal: they establish the pattern and test for long-range associations against a null that preserves each chromosome's structure.
