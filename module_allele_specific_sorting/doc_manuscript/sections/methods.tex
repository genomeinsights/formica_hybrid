\subsection{Rationale}

A locus can be strongly differentiated among populations whenever the populations differ in allele frequency at that locus. LD among populations, in contrast, arises only when two loci differ among the \emph{same} populations in a consistent direction. High \FST\ at many loci therefore implies high multilocus LD only if the loci share their population partition. If ancestry has sorted at different loci in different subsets of populations, strong differentiation can accumulate across the genome while LD remains close to what linkage alone maintains. We refer to this as locus-specific (heterogeneous) ancestry sorting.

\subsection{Units, populations and orientation}

The distance analyses used the 20,807 LD-reduced units of the DI25 panel (51,612 markers with parental diagnostic index $>-25$; from-scratch hybrids-only clustering with a 5~cM merge cap and the decay-relative Stage-2 quality gate $\rho=0.5$), represented by their best SNP (clusters of more than two markers) or representative SNP, using strictly observed genotypes. Of these units, 5,787 combine several SNPs (up to 2,991). Combining highly correlated nearby markers avoids counting one correlated region many times, but it also removes part of the short-range LD that the analyses examine; the neighbourhood analysis was therefore repeated with all 51,612 unpruned SNPs. Genotypes of the 165 hybrid individuals in 20 populations were oriented so that the allele dosage counts the \Fa\ allele, as identified from the parental reference samples. For each marker $i$ and population $k$ we computed the \Fa-allele frequency $F_{ik}$; its among-population profile is the basis for the among-population statistics below. Per-unit differentiation was estimated with the \citet{weir1984estimating} \FST\ among the 20 hybrid populations. Directional sorting was classified with the convention used throughout the project (near-fixation at $\phi=0.85$, sorting threshold $\tau=0.6$, binomial test of direction at $\alpha=0.05$), giving 1,215 units sorted toward \Fa\ and 365 toward \Fp.

\subsection{Pairwise statistics}

\paragraph{Ancestry-profile similarity (concordance).} For markers $i$ and $j$, $c_{ij}$ is the Pearson correlation of their oriented profiles across the 20 populations. Because profiles are oriented, the sign is interpretable: a positive value means that the populations enriched for \Fa\ ancestry at one locus are also enriched at the other. We also computed concordance after residualising each profile on a leave-one-chromosome-out estimate of each population's genome-wide ancestry, which removes the shared ancestry gradient among populations.

\paragraph{Among-population LD and its differentiation ceiling.} Following the decomposition of LD in subdivided populations by \citet{ohta1982linkage}, the among-population component of LD between two loci, normalised as a correlation, is
\begin{equation}
r_{ST,ij}=\frac{\operatorname{cov}_k(F_{ik},F_{jk})}{\sqrt{\bar F_i(1-\bar F_i)\,\bar F_j(1-\bar F_j)}}=c_{ij}\sqrt{G_iG_j},
\qquad G_i=\frac{\operatorname{var}_k(F_{ik})}{\bar F_i(1-\bar F_i)},
\label{eq:rst}
\end{equation}
where $G_i$ is the (Nei-type) among-population differentiation of locus $i$, with means and variances taken over the 20 populations with equal weight. Equation~\eqref{eq:rst} separates the two ingredients of among-population LD. The product $G_iG_j$ is a \emph{ceiling}: the value $r_{ST,ij}^2$ would take if the two loci partitioned the populations identically ($|c_{ij}|=1$). It depends only on the marginal differentiation of each locus. The \emph{realised fraction} of the ceiling, summarised over a set of pairs as $\sum r_{ST}^2/\sum G_iG_j$, is the $G$-weighted mean of $c_{ij}^2$.

\paragraph{Population-label permutation baseline.} Two loci with no shared partition still have non-zero $c_{ij}^2$ because only 20 populations are sampled (expected value $1/19$). We obtained the baseline for each distance class by permuting the 20 population labels independently for every unit (500 permutations), which keeps each unit's profile and differentiation, and therefore the ceiling, unchanged while removing every correspondence between units. For cross-chromosome pairs (206.8 million) the permuted sums were computed exactly from per-chromosome $20\times20$ matrices, $\sum_{i\in a,j\in b}c_{ij}^2g_ig_j=\langle M_a,M_b\rangle$ with $M_a=\sum_{i\in a}g_i\mathbf{z}_i\mathbf{z}_i^\top$ for unit-norm standardised profiles $\mathbf{z}_i$; unpermuted values reproduced the explicit pairwise sums exactly.

\paragraph{Within-population LD.} Within-population LD $r_w$ is the correlation between oriented genotypes after centring each marker within each population, pooled over individuals (a sample-size-weighted within-population correlation), after additionally regressing each marker, within populations, on each individual's leave-one-chromosome-out hybrid index. The adjustment removes admixture LD generated by variation in overall ancestry among individuals of the same population; it changed no reported value at the precision shown, and only the adjusted statistic is reported. Missing genotypes (2.7\%) were set to the population mean. A pooled correlation is diluted whenever one marker is monomorphic in a population, which contributes to the other marker's variance but not to the covariance. Because sorted loci are, by definition, near-fixed in most populations, the comparison of sorted and unsorted loci used $r_w^{\mathrm{poly}}$, the pooled correlation restricted, for each pair, to populations in which both markers segregate.

\subsection{Distance analysis}

All 9.6 million within-chromosome pairs of units and all 206.8 million cross-chromosome pairs were evaluated. Pairs were binned by genetic distance on the per-chromosome recombination map (headline analysis) and, as a sensitivity analysis, by physical distance; cross-chromosome pairs formed an additional ``unlinked'' class. Uncertainty was estimated with a chromosome-block bootstrap (2,000 replicates) that resampled the 26 chromosomes with replacement. Within-chromosome pairs were weighted by the number of times their chromosome was drawn, and cross-chromosome pairs between chromosomes $a$ and $b$ by the product of the two draw counts, so that pairs between two copies of the same chromosome were never treated as unlinked.

\subsection{Neighbourhoods of sorted loci}

If sorting had acted on long haplotypes --- for example through selective sweeps, or through retention of long ancestry blocks around selected loci --- the markers near sorted loci should share their population partition, and their within-population LD, more than the markers near equally differentiated unsorted loci. We tested this by comparing each sorted locus (anchor) with up to five unsorted loci matched on standardised \FST, $\log_{10}$ local recombination rate, parental allele-frequency difference and local unit density (units within $\pm100$~kb, which determines how many close neighbours a locus can have). Matching on \FST\ is necessary because concordance increases with \FST\ genome-wide. Controls were the nearest unsorted loci within a caliper of 1~SD on every covariate. Narrower calipers were examined (0.25 and 0.5~SD) but excluded 37\% and 10\% of sorted loci, disproportionately the largest sorted LD blocks, and gave worse covariate balance; with 1~SD, 1,552 of the 1,577 sorted loci with complete covariates were matched and all standardised mean differences were $\leq0.04$ in absolute value (Table~\ref{tab:balance}). The 25 unmatched sorted loci are reported separately (Table~\ref{tab:unmatched}).

For each anchor and control, ancestry-profile similarity and $r_w^{\mathrm{poly}}$ were averaged over the neighbouring markers within each distance class up to 2~Mb, using two marker sets: (i) the representative SNPs of other LD-reduced units, and (ii) all unpruned DI25 SNPs, including SNPs in the anchor's own LD cluster, where an extended haplotype carrying the sorted allele would be visible. We also compared the size (number of SNPs) and physical span of each locus's own LD unit. For every anchor we computed the difference between its value and the mean of its own controls, and averaged these matched-set differences over anchors. Confidence intervals came from a chromosome-block bootstrap (2,000 replicates) that resampled the anchors' chromosomes and kept each matched set intact.

\subsection{Relation to the neutral simulations}

The comparisons above are internal: they describe how differentiation and LD are distributed along the genome and compare sorted loci with matched unsorted loci. They do not establish that these patterns differ from those produced by neutral admixture and drift.
