\subsection{High differentiation, but shared population partitions mainly over linkage distances}

Ancestry-informative units were strongly differentiated among the hybrid populations (median \FST\ $=0.25$; mean Nei-type $G=0.30$). Consequently, the among-population LD that pairs of units could show if they partitioned the populations identically --- the ceiling $G_iG_j$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:rst} --- was high, 0.090--0.093, and, as expected because it depends only on the loci's marginal differentiation, constant from the closest pairs to pairs on different chromosomes (Figure~\ref{fig:decay}a).

Observed among-population LD was a small fraction of this ceiling, and the fraction declined with genetic distance (Figure~\ref{fig:decay}b). It was highest for pairs 0.001--0.01~cM apart (0.217 [95\% CI 0.203, 0.233], against a permutation baseline of 0.053), 0.148 at 0.05--0.2~cM, 0.084 at 0.2--1~cM and 0.065 at 1--5~cM. Beyond 5~cM (0.058) and for unlinked pairs (0.0571 [0.0568, 0.0574]) it remained close to, but measurably above, the baseline of 0.053; the excess for unlinked pairs (0.004) reflects the shared gradient in genome-wide ancestry among populations: raw concordance between unlinked units was 0.033, whereas residualised concordance was $-0.0004$ [$-0.0007$, $-0.0000$]. Residualised concordance declined from 0.330 at 0.001--0.01~cM to 0.103 at 0.2--1~cM, 0.041 at 1--5~cM and 0.004 beyond 5~cM. Within-population LD declined in parallel, from $r_w^2=0.159$ at 0.001--0.01~cM to 0.025 at 1--5~cM and 0.010 beyond 5~cM, against 0.008 for unlinked pairs.

Pairs less than 0.001~cM apart departed from this monotonic decline: their realised fraction (0.153), residualised concordance (0.231) and within-population LD (0.097) were lower than at 0.001--0.01~cM. This class contains pairs that are physically distant but lie within recombination cold spots, where the interpolated map is flat; it is therefore more heterogeneous in physical distance than the others. On the physical scale (Figure~\ref{fig:decay-bp}) the decline was monotonic, from a realised fraction of 0.243 within 5~kb to 0.073 at 20--100~kb and 0.058 at 0.5--2~Mb.

Thus, the ancestry-informative genome is broadly and strongly differentiated, but different regions --- beyond the scale of linkage --- usually distinguish different subsets of populations. High marginal differentiation therefore coexists with among-population LD that is substantial only between linked markers.

\subsection{Sorted loci do not share their sorting with nearby markers more than matched unsorted loci}

The matched controls were closely balanced with the sorted loci on all matching covariates (Table~\ref{tab:balance}; Figure~\ref{fig:balance}). Across both marker sets, sorted loci did not share their ancestry pattern or their within-population LD with nearby markers more than their matched controls did at any distance (Table~\ref{tab:neighbourhood}; Figure~\ref{fig:neighbourhood}).

Ancestry-profile similarity with markers within 5~kb was lower around sorted loci than around their controls, both for LD-reduced units (0--1~kb: $-0.057$ [$-0.088$, $-0.028$]; 1--5~kb: $-0.073$ [$-0.094$, $-0.050$]) and for unpruned SNPs ($-0.073$ [$-0.104$, $-0.049$] and $-0.061$ [$-0.085$, $-0.038$]). At 5--20~kb the difference was small ($-0.018$ for units, $-0.011$ for SNPs), and from 20~kb to 2~Mb it was indistinguishable from zero. Within-population LD among segregating markers was equal to or slightly lower than around controls: the differences were within $\pm0.03$ at all distances, with intervals excluding zero only where sorted loci were lower (LD-reduced units at 1--5~kb, $-0.029$ [$-0.051$, $-0.006$]; unpruned SNPs at 0--1~kb, $-0.026$ [$-0.057$, $-0.0003$] and at 0.5--2~Mb, $-0.006$ [$-0.012$, $-0.002$]). The LD units of sorted loci were not larger than those of their controls (difference in $\log_2$ SNPs 0.027 [$-0.044$, 0.088]) and spanned a shorter physical distance (13~kb versus 26~kb; difference $-12.7$~kb [$-29.7$, $-0.1$]).

The test therefore had the opportunity to detect extended haplotypes directly --- the unpruned analysis includes every SNP in a sorted locus's own LD cluster --- and found none in excess of what equally differentiated unsorted loci in comparable genomic contexts show. This is consistent with locus-specific sorting rather than sorting through long, consistently inherited ancestry blocks. Two qualifications apply. First, 25 sorted loci had no unsorted counterpart within the matching caliper (Table~\ref{tab:unmatched}). They are, on average, much larger LD blocks (mean 121 SNPs and 907~kb, against 3.2 SNPs and 13~kb for matched sorted loci), typically in regions of lower-than-average recombination (median 8.7 versus 21.8~cM/Mb genome-wide; 7 below 1~cM/Mb), and include blocks of several megabases on chromosomes 16, 1, 17 and 26. In these regions sorting has, by construction, acted on long haplotypes, and the matched comparison cannot assess them. Second, the short-range similarity deficit means that, at sorted loci, the population pattern sometimes differs even from that of very close neighbours. Its cause has not been resolved; it may partly reflect the sorting classification itself, which requires near-fixation that an equally differentiated but unsorted neighbour by definition lacks.
