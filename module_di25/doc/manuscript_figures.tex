\figblock{diem_circos_compare_snp_vs_ldreduced.png}
{Figure 1 --- Ancestry mosaic before and after LD complexity reduction}
{% caption
\textbf{Genome-wide DIEM ancestry mosaic of the diagnostic markers, per individual,
before and after LD complexity reduction.} Circular genotype heatmaps of 195 individuals ---
165 hybrid workers and 30 parental reference individuals: each concentric ring is one
individual, and the rings are ordered once --- innermost = most \Faq{}, outermost = most
\Fpo{}, by the per-SNP mean hybrid index --- with that same order applied to both panels, so a
ring at a given radius is the same individual in \textbf{(a)} and \textbf{(b)}. Markers are
arranged by genomic position around the circle (chromosomes~1--27). Cell colour is DIEM
ancestry: purple = homozygous \Faq{}, teal = heterozygous, yellow = homozygous \Fpo{}; missing
genotypes are blank. \textbf{(a)} All 51{,}612 diagnostic SNPs. \textbf{(b)} The same
individuals and markers after LD complexity reduction to 11{,}052 units at a 5\,cM merge cap
(4{,}010 best-SNP units --- the consensus-optimal member per cluster of $>2$ markers --- plus
7{,}042 representative SNPs from 1--2-marker clusters). The large-scale ancestry structure --- the per-individual
\Faq{}--\Fpo{} gradient and the chromosomal ancestry blocks --- is retained after the
$\sim$4.7-fold reduction, with the redundant within-block SNP replication collapsed.}
{% methods
\emph{Data.} The same DI\,$>-25$ diagnostic markers and LD-reduced units as Figure~2
(51{,}612 SNPs; 11{,}052 units), displayed across all 195 genotyped individuals --- 165
\Faq{}\,$\times$\,\Fpo{} hybrid workers from 20 populations and 30 parental references (15 per
species). Genotypes are shown in three states --- homozygous \Faq{}, heterozygous, homozygous
\Fpo{} --- with missing calls left blank.

\emph{Units (panel b).} Each LD-reduced unit is represented by a single real SNP:
clusters of more than two markers by their best-SNP (the member whose genotype best matches
the cluster's eMLG consensus), 1--2-marker clusters by their representative SNP. Each unit was oriented to the
\Faq{} allele by the sign of the parental allele-frequency difference and then discretised to
the three-state ancestry code; units are placed at their representative marker's genomic
position.

\emph{Individual order.} Individuals were ordered once by their per-SNP mean hybrid index
(fraction \Faq{}) and that single order was applied to both panels, so LD reduction is the
only difference between a and b.

\emph{Rendering.} Both panels are drawn as polar rasters
(\texttt{render\_diem\_circos()}, \texttt{module\_di25/R/diem\_circos\_core.R}) and written as
one 5200\,$\times$\,2750-pixel, 300-dpi PNG by
\texttt{module\_di25/R/diem\_circos\_compare.R}. Being dense per-cell rasters
(individuals\,$\times$\,tens of thousands of loci), the panels are emitted as a
high-resolution bitmap rather than vector graphics.}
{% note
\emph{Colour scheme differs from the line figures.} In this ancestry heatmap teal marks
\emph{heterozygotes} and the \Faq{}/\Fpo{} homozygotes are purple/yellow (the three-state
DIEM coding). In Figure~2 the teal/yellow pair instead marks the \emph{direction} of
sorting (\Faq{}/\Fpo{}). The consistent anchor across figures is yellow\,=\,\Fpo{}.}

\figblock{fig_recomb_directional.pdf}
{Figure 2 --- Direction of ancestry sorting vs recombination}
{% caption
\textbf{Ancestry sorting toward each parental species as a function of recombination, and
its dependence on the sorting threshold.}
\textbf{(a)} Fraction of units sorted toward \Faq{} (teal) or
\Fpo{} (yellow) across recombination deciles (\emph{x}: median cM/Mb per decile), at two
sorting thresholds --- a unit is ``sorted'' when at least 60\,\% (left) or 80\,\% (right)
of populations are near-fixed. Solid lines, LD-reduced units (eMLG); dashed lines,
individual SNPs. Grey bars give the number of independent eMLG units per decile (right
axis). The three lowest-recombination deciles (shaded) contain only 8, 9 and 38 independent
units, so their per-decile \emph{fractions} are individually imprecise --- a couple of units
shift them substantially; they are shaded for that reason, not because the underlying signal is
doubtful. The directional contrast at low recombination is therefore quantified in panel b,
which enters each unit as one observation along the continuous gradient rather than reading a
fraction off these sparse bins. Error bars are Wilson 95\,\% confidence intervals, shown for the
eMLG estimates only (SNPs are not independent). \textbf{(b)} Recombination coefficient of
each direction's sorting at each threshold: the slope of a logistic regression of
$P(\text{sorted toward the species})$ on $\log_{10}$ recombination, fitted across all
LD-reduced units; points and 95\,\% intervals are from a genomic block bootstrap that
resamples whole chromosomes. Negative values indicate sorting concentrated at low
recombination. Both directions concentrate at low recombination, but \Fpo{}-directed sorting
does so more strongly: the paired block-bootstrap difference
in coefficient (\Faq{}\,$-$\,\Fpo{}) is positive at all four thresholds and its 95\,\% CI
excludes zero at three of them ($\tau=0.6$: $+1.1$, 95\,\% CI $[0.05,\,2.2]$, two-sided
$p=0.04$; significant at $\tau=0.5$--$0.7$, marginal at $\tau=0.8$ where $p=0.06$, shown above
each threshold in panel b). Fitting the gradient this way is what makes
the low-recombination contrast estimable: the sparse bins of panel a cannot support a stable
per-decile fraction, whereas the regression enters every unit --- including the few large
\Fpo{}-sorted low-recombination blocks --- as one independent observation, with the
chromosome-level block bootstrap accounting for their non-independence.}
{% methods
\emph{Scope.} The entire pipeline underlying this figure --- LD-decay estimation, LD-based
complexity reduction, and the ancestry-sorting analysis --- is based exclusively on the
high-diagnostic-index (DI $>-25$) markers; other analyses in this study use the full marker
set.

\emph{Diagnostic markers and populations.} We analysed the 51{,}612 species-diagnostic
SNPs (diagnostic index $>-25$) genotyped in 165 \Faq{}\,$\times$\,\Fpo{} hybrid workers
from 20 populations, with 30 parental reference individuals, coded as 0/1/2 allele dosage.

\emph{LD-reduced units.} Linkage-disequilibrium clustering was rebuilt from scratch on the
diagnostic markers only, using hybrids alone (including parents would inflate LD through
parent--hybrid structure): chromosome-specific LD-decay, a per-marker local-LD statistic,
and a two-stage, quality-gated complexity reduction with a 0.5\,cM Stage-1 threshold and a
5\,cM merge cap. This collapsed the 51{,}612 SNPs to 11{,}052 LD-reduced units; clusters of
more than two markers are represented by their best-SNP (the member whose genotype best matches
the cluster's eMLG consensus), and 1--2-marker clusters by their representative SNP.

\emph{Ancestry sorting.} Each unit was oriented to the \Faq{} allele by the sign of the
parental allele-frequency difference. A population was scored near-fixed toward a species
when its oriented allele frequency reached $\varphi=0.85$ (\Faq{}) or fell to $\le 1-\varphi$
(\Fpo{}). A unit was called sorted when the proportion of populations near-fixed reached the
threshold $\tau$; among sorted units the direction was assigned to the majority species when
a two-sided binomial test of $n_{\text{aqu}}$ among $n_{\text{fixed}}$ ($\alpha=0.05$)
rejected parity, and left unresolved otherwise. The only diagnosticity gate is the
DI\,$>-25$ marker selection itself; unlike the genome-wide Module-A sorting, no additional
pooled-parental MAF\,$\ge 0.15$ filter is applied, since restricting to DI\,$>-25$ already
removes the near-monomorphic loci that filter guards against (it dropped only $\sim$398 of
51{,}612 SNPs). The same statistics were computed at the SNP level for comparison.

\emph{Recombination.} Recombination rate (cM/Mb) was assigned to each marker (and each
unit's representative position) by per-chromosome linear interpolation of the linkage map.
Recombination deciles were defined on the SNP distribution and applied to both levels.

\emph{Panel a.} Within each recombination decile we computed the fraction of differentiated
units (and SNPs) sorted toward each species at $\tau=0.6$ and $\tau=0.8$; grey bars show the
per-decile count of independent eMLG units, deciles with $<100$ units were shaded as
unreliable, and Wilson 95\,\% confidence intervals are shown for eMLG estimates.

\emph{Panel b.} For each $\tau\in\{0.5,0.6,0.7,0.8\}$ and each direction we fitted a logistic
regression of the binary ``sorted toward that species'' indicator on
$\log_{10}(\text{recombination}+0.1)$ across all LD-reduced units. Because units are only
approximately independent, 95\,\% confidence intervals for the recombination coefficient
were obtained from a genomic block bootstrap resampling whole chromosomes (1000 replicates).
The two directions were then compared with a \emph{paired} version of the same bootstrap:
within each replicate the identical resampled chromosomes were used to refit both directions
and their coefficient difference was recorded. The 95\,\% CI of that paired difference and a
two-sided $p$ (twice the fraction of replicates in which the difference reversed sign) test
equality of the two directions' recombination dependence; these $p$-values are printed above
each threshold in panel b.}
{% note
\emph{What the shaded low-recombination deciles actually contain --- counts, not a fraction ---
and why this figure is preliminary.} The three lowest deciles hold 55 units in all; most are
directionally unresolved, and the sorted signal is a handful of large blocks that each collapse
to a single LD-reduced unit. Toward \Fpo{} these are a 350-marker block on chromosome~26, a
190-marker block on chromosome~5 and a 72-marker block on chromosome~6, whose member SNPs
individually reach near-fixation. Toward \Faq{} there is an 89-marker block on chromosome~15
and --- at the more permissive $\tau=0.6$ --- a 384-marker block on chromosome~13, so the
low-recombination \Faq{} signal is \emph{not} confined to tiny clusters. That 384-marker block
is provisional: its pooled-parental MAF ($0.137$) sits just below the $0.15$ floor used by the
genome-wide Module-A sorting, and it enters here only because that gate is deliberately not
applied to the DI25 set (the DI\,$>-25$ selection is itself the gate). Because the
low-recombination contrast rests on so few large units it is sensitive to their handful: it is
significant at $\tau=0.5$--$0.7$ but only marginal at $\tau=0.8$ ($p=0.06$). All of this will be
revisited when the diagnostic indices are reseeded (these analyses now use the corrected
195-individual sample).

\emph{This does not contradict the all-marker (Module~A) result.} Module~A finds sorting
\emph{depleted} at low recombination (low recombination holds few independent units, most washing
out); the enrichment here is specific to \emph{diagnostic} (DI\,$>-25$) units. The difference is
the DI restriction alone, not the clustering: gating Module~A's own units to their
\emph{representative} DI\,$>-25$ flips its recombination slope from $+0.6$ to $-1.1$, reproducing
this figure, whereas changing the merge cap (0.5 vs 5\,cM) or the MAF gate leaves it unchanged.
And because DI is estimated from the same hybrid data, restricting to high-DI loci partly selects
the ancestry-informative units that sort, so this low-recombination enrichment is a statement
about diagnostic loci --- not a reversal of the all-marker depletion.}

\figblock{fig_size_directional.pdf}
{Figure 3 --- Among diagnostic units, sorting direction covaries with LD-cluster size}
{% caption
\textbf{Among sorted units, direction is predicted by LD-cluster size more strongly than by
recombination; recombination instead predicts whether a unit sorts at all.} Unit level
($\tau=0.8$ in a--b; SNPs are all single-marker).
\textbf{(a)} Fraction of units sorted toward \Faq{} (teal) or \Fpo{} (yellow) by cluster-size
class: \Faq{}-directed predominates among small clusters, \Fpo{}-directed among large ones. The
top classes are sparse --- essentially the $\sim$12 large blocks named in
Fig.~2's note (F2233 chr13, F7174 chr26, F6986 chr25, F8626/F8627 chr5, \dots), re-binned by
size. \textbf{(b)} The same relationship without binning: each sorted unit is a point at its
cluster size (log scale), at $y=0$ if sorted toward \Faq{} or $y=1$ toward \Fpo{}, with the
logistic fit $P(\Fpo\mid\text{sorted}) \sim \log_{10}(\text{size})$ and its chromosome
block-bootstrap 95\,\% ribbon. Plotting the units directly shows how sparse the large-cluster
tail is: almost all sorted units are small and \Faq{}-directed, and the rise toward \Fpo{} is
carried by a scatter of a few large blocks --- not an independent axis escaping Fig.~2's
sparsity. \textbf{(c)} A single model, conditional on sorting:
$P(\Fpo\mid\text{sorted}) \sim \log_{10}(\text{recombination}) + \log_{10}(\text{cluster size})$,
fitted across all sorted units per $\tau$ (chromosome block bootstrap, 1000 replicates), on both
two single-SNP representations of each unit: its best-SNP (the consensus-optimal member; filled)
and its centrality representative (the highest-median-$r^2$ member, from the per-SNP analysis;
open). \emph{Cluster size is the stronger predictor of direction}: its coefficient is positive
with a 95\,\% CI excluding zero on \emph{both} representations (best-SNP $+0.6$ at $\tau=0.6$,
$+1.2$ at $\tau=0.8$; centrality representative $+0.5$/$+1.4$), so the size--direction effect does
not depend on which single member represents the cluster. Recombination has a direction effect of
the \emph{same} sign, but its 95\,\% CI now includes zero once size is entered ($-0.4$ at
$\tau=0.6$), because the two predictors are collinear among the sorted units ($r\approx-0.3$) and
size carries more of the shared signal. Recombination is thus not eliminated as a direction
predictor; size is simply the stronger one.}
{% methods
\emph{Data and units.} The same DI\,$>-25$ units as Figure~2 (no MAF gate), unit level. Cluster
size is the number of diagnostic markers per unit; recombination is the map-interpolated rate
(cM/Mb) at the unit's representative marker. The two correlate weakly across all units
(Pearson $-0.19$ on the logs) but appreciably among the sorted units the direction model is
fitted on ($\approx-0.3$).

\emph{Two single-SNP representations.} \emph{best-SNP} scores each unit from its consensus-optimal
member --- the member whose genotype best matches the cluster's eMLG consensus (representative SNP
for 1--2-marker units), as in Fig.~2; \emph{centrality representative} scores each unit from its
representative marker, the highest-median-$r^2$ member (from the per-SNP analysis). Both are single
real SNPs; neither averages over members, so the comparison probes whether the direction effect
depends on which member represents the cluster.

\emph{Panels a--b ($\tau=0.8$).} Panel a bins units by size class (fraction sorted toward each
species, Wilson 95\,\% CIs). Panel b plots each sorted unit at its cluster size with the logistic
fit $P(\Fpo\mid\text{sorted}) \sim \log_{10}(\text{size})$; its ribbon is the same chromosome
block bootstrap as panel c, so b and c share their inference.

\emph{Panel c.} Among units classified as sorted (either direction) we fitted, per
$\tau\in\{0.5,0.6,0.7,0.8\}$ and per basis, a logistic regression of the binary \Fpo{}-vs-\Faq{}
outcome on $\log_{10}(\text{recombination}+0.1)$ and $\log_{10}(\text{cluster size})$ jointly;
95\,\% CIs are from a genomic block bootstrap resampling whole chromosomes (1000 replicates).}
{% note
\emph{Recombination predicts \emph{whether} a unit sorts; cluster size predicts \emph{which}
parent --- and Fig.~2b is the two confounded.} Fitted unconditionally across all units (not just
sorted ones), the propensity to sort is strongly and independently predicted by recombination
($P(\text{sorted}) \sim \log_{10}$ recombination $= -1.3$ at $\tau=0.6$, $-1.9$ at $\tau=0.8$; CI
excludes zero, and it \emph{strengthens} when size is added): more sorting at low recombination.
Cluster size instead predicts the direction (panel c). Because Figure~2b is unconditional and
single-predictor, it mixes the two: its recombination--direction signal is largely this
propensity effect showing through, which is why Fig.~2b's paired difference and panel c's
conditional size effect agree in sign. Figure~2b therefore \emph{motivates} this figure rather
than being refuted by it.

The size--direction effect holds on both single-SNP representations (panel c), so it does not
depend on how the cluster is summarised --- neither the consensus-optimal member nor the centrality
representative is an averaged value, and the effect survives swapping one for the other. All
preliminary pending the reseeded-DI rerun (see Fig.~2).

\emph{Caveat --- this size$\to$direction pattern is specific to the diagnostic set and does not
generalise.} Refitting the direction model on Module~A's all-marker units (same clustering
machinery; sorting on either the representative or the best-SNP) \emph{reverses} the size
coefficient (larger clusters then lean \Faq{}, $\approx-0.3$ to $-0.5$); the positive DI25 effect
appears only once clustering is restricted to diagnostic markers, where the large clusters
\emph{are} the polyctena centromeric blocks. Panel c is therefore best read as a property of the
diagnostic-block structure --- in this set sorting is carried by a few large low-recombination
blocks (chr26/5/6 toward \Fpo{}, chr13/15 toward \Faq{}) --- not as a general size$\to$direction
mechanism.}

\figblock{bdmi_sorting_circos_cutoff5.png}
{Figure 4 --- BDMI candidate regions coincide with ancestry-sorted, low-recombination regions}
{% caption
\textbf{Genomic regions carrying candidate hybrid incompatibilities (BDMIs) sit on the
ancestry-sorted, low-recombination parts of the genome, not the unsorted background.} Three
aligned tracks share one polar geometry (chromosomes~1--27 by diagnostic-SNP rank; arc
$\propto$ number of diagnostic SNPs). \textbf{Middle:} an ancestry-sorting $\tau$ sweep (four
rings, $\tau=0.5$--$0.8$ inner$\to$outer; each diagnostic SNP coloured by sort class --- \Faq{}
teal, \Fpo{} yellow, direction unresolved purple, unsorted near-white). \textbf{BDMI ring:}
diagnostic SNPs inside a BDMI candidate region (the $\chi^2\ge 0.05$ set, 58 merged regions),
coloured \textbf{blue} where that SNP is itself ancestry-sorted (at $\tau=0.6$) and
\textbf{orange} otherwise --- the blue segments (chr~25, 11, 15, 17, 24) mark where the BDMI
regions overlap sorting rather than lying on unsorted background. \textbf{Outermost ring (magenta):}
diagnostic SNPs in a low-recombination window (LD-decay rate close to zero; see Methods). The three
tracks coincide: the blue BDMI-overlap segments fall on the sorted blocks, which in turn sit on
the magenta low-recombination blocks.}
{% methods
\emph{BDMI candidate regions.} An independent between-population DMI screen (collaborator
data) tested pairs of loci in male hybrids of one \Faq{}\,$\times$\,\Fpo{} hybrid population
for distorted two-locus genotype frequencies; loci belonging to at least one significant
pair at a given $\chi^2$ statistic cutoff were exported as genomic intervals and lifted onto
the \emph{Frufa\_DTOL\_PR} reference used here (23 nested cutoffs, $\chi^2 = 0.005$--$0.06$).
Each file is the union of participating loci as merged intervals (the pairing that would draw
edges between them is not retained), so this analysis treats them as candidate \emph{regions},
not as an incompatibility network. Overlapping intervals were merged per chromosome.

\emph{Rings.} Using the same marker order and chromosome geometry as Figure~1 and the same sort
classification as Figure~2 (four thresholds $\tau=0.5$--$0.8$, $\varphi=0.85$, binomial direction
test at $\alpha=0.05$; DI\,$>-25$ SNP set, $n=51{,}612$), each diagnostic SNP was flagged as
inside/outside the BDMI regions at the chosen cutoff and, if inside, coloured by whether it is
also sorted at $\tau=0.6$ (blue) or not (orange). The outermost low-recombination ring uses
per-chromosome sliding-window LD decay ($\sim$500-kb windows; \texttt{di25\_ld\_decay.rds}), where each window
carries an exponential decay rate $a$ in $r^2 \approx c\,e^{-a d}+b$: windows with $a$ close to
zero ($a<10^{-5}$, i.e. LD half-decay $>\sim$70\,kb --- slow decay, high LD, low recombination)
were merged and every diagnostic SNP inside one marked. Rendered by
\texttt{module\_di25/R/bdmi\_sorting\_circos.R} (\texttt{CUTOFF} selects the cutoff, \texttt{LD\_A}
the decay-rate cut) as a polar raster over \texttt{render\_circos\_raster()}.}
{% note
\emph{Coordinates are shared.} The colleague's regions and this study's diagnostic markers are
both on the \emph{Frufa\_DTOL\_PR} assembly, so base-pair positions are directly comparable; no
further liftover was applied on our side. Chromosome~23 carries no diagnostic markers and is
absent in both. This figure is a qualitative, three-track overview; the BDMI\,$\leftrightarrow$\,sorting
overlap is quantified in Figure~5, its independence from recombination architecture in
Figures~10--11.}

\figblock{bdmi_sorting_overlap.png}
{Figure 5 --- BDMI regions are enriched for sorted loci, most strongly toward \Fpo{}}
{% caption
\textbf{Diagnostic SNPs inside BDMI candidate regions are several-fold more likely to be
ancestry-sorted than expected under a position-randomised null, and the enrichment is
carried disproportionately by \Fpo{}-directed sorting.} For each of the 23 $\chi^2$ cutoffs
(\emph{x}, log scale; more stringent cutoffs to the right retain fewer, stronger regions),
\textbf{(a)} the fold-enrichment of sorted diagnostic SNPs inside the BDMI regions relative
to the rotation null --- for any-direction sorting (blue), \Fpo{}-directed (yellow) and
\Faq{}-directed (teal) --- and \textbf{(b)} the corresponding rotation-null $p$
($-\log_{10}$; dashed line $p=0.05$). At stringent cutoffs the overlap is strong and highly
significant (any-sorted up to $5.7\times$, $p\approx5\times10^{-4}$ --- the 2000-permutation
floor; \Fpo{}-directed up to $8.7\times$), and it decays to $\sim\!1\times$ (no signal) as the
cutoff relaxes and the regions blanket $\sim$2/3 of the genome. Across all cutoffs the
\Fpo{}-directed enrichment exceeds the \Faq{}-directed one.}
{% methods
\emph{Test.} Each diagnostic SNP (DI\,$>-25$; $n=51{,}612$) was classified sorted/unsorted and
by direction at $\tau=0.6$, $\varphi=0.85$ (as in Fig.~2), and flagged inside/outside the merged
BDMI regions at each cutoff. Enrichment is the observed count of sorted in-region SNPs divided by
its null mean.

\emph{Rotation null.} Significance is from a per-chromosome circular-rotation null (2000
permutations): within each chromosome the diagnostic-SNP coordinates were shifted by a random
offset and wrapped, holding fixed both that chromosome's sorted-direction pattern and its total
BDMI coverage, and randomising only the \emph{phase} of the regions relative to the SNPs. A
positive result therefore indicates that the \emph{actual} region positions coincide with sorted
loci beyond chance placement, not merely that both favour the same chromosomes. Empirical
one-sided $p=(1+\#\{\text{null}\ge\text{obs}\})/(1+2000)$. Generated by
\texttt{module\_di25/R/bdmi\_sorting\_overlap.R}.}
{% note
\emph{Not driven by the three large \Fpo{} blocks.} Repeating the test with chromosomes~5, 25
and 26 --- the big low-recombination \Fpo{} blocks (Figs.~2--3) --- removed entirely (9{,}086
SNPs dropped; the \Fpo{}-sorted pool falls from 2{,}102 to 787 loci) \emph{strengthens} the
result: the stringent-cutoff \Fpo{} enrichment rises to $10.4\times$ and becomes significant
across almost the whole cutoff range rather than only the stringent end
(\texttt{bdmi\_sorting\_overlap\_no5\_25\_26.png}). Those blocks are so large and near-fixed that
they add variance to the null without contributing resolvable phase information; the
BDMI\,$\leftrightarrow$\,sorting coupling is genome-wide and block-independent.

\emph{Shared-power caveat.} A DMI screen based on allele-frequency / LD contrasts has more power
where ancestry is differentiated, which is also where sorting is detected; the rotation null
controls for genomic \emph{position} but not for this shared dependence on differentiation, so
part of the overlap may be a power coincidence rather than a causal link. Because the input
regions are the union of participating loci (edges not retained), this figure speaks to
\emph{co-location} of BDMI candidates and sorting, not to specific incompatible pairs. Preliminary
pending the reseeded-DI rerun (see Fig.~2).}

\figblock{bdmi_sorting_null_hist_cutoff5.png}
{Figure 5---figure supplement 1 --- The rotation null behind the overlap test}
{% caption
\textbf{The permutation test underlying Figure~5, shown as null distributions for one
representative cutoff.} For the $\chi^2\ge 0.05$ region set (58 regions; 2{,}509 diagnostic
SNPs inside), each panel is the per-chromosome circular-rotation null distribution
(5{,}000 permutations) of the number of sorted diagnostic SNPs falling inside the BDMI
regions --- \textbf{(left)} any-direction sorted, \textbf{(centre)} toward \Fpo{},
\textbf{(right)} toward \Faq{}. The coloured vertical line is the \emph{observed} count; in
every panel it lies far in the right tail of the null (any-sorted $878$ vs null mean $207$,
fold $4.2\times$, $p<2\times10^{-4}$; \Fpo{} $379$ vs $62$, fold $6.1\times$, $p=0.006$;
\Faq{} $456$ vs $143$, fold $3.2\times$, $p=0.0004$). The \Fpo{} null is strongly
right-skewed --- rotations that happen to place regions on the large clustered \Fpo{} blocks
generate the long tail --- yet the observed value still exceeds it.}
{% methods
\emph{Test.} Identical to Figure~5 but for the single cutoff $\chi^2=0.05$ and with $5{,}000$
permutations (a single cutoff, so more permutations for a smooth null). Each permutation
circularly shifts the diagnostic-SNP coordinates within each chromosome by a random offset,
holding each chromosome's sorted-direction pattern and BDMI coverage fixed and randomising only
the phase; the statistic is the count of sorted (respectively \Fpo{}-/\Faq{}-directed) SNPs
inside the regions. Empirical one-sided $p=(1+\#\{\text{null}\ge\text{obs}\})/(1+5000)$.
Generated by \texttt{module\_di25/R/bdmi\_sorting\_null\_hist.R} (\texttt{CUTOFF},
\texttt{EXCLUDE\_CHR}, \texttt{N\_PERM} selectable).}

\figblock{di25_sorting_emp_vs_sim.pdf}
{Figure 9 --- Ancestry sorting exceeds a demographic null that reproduces the LD landscape}
{% caption
\textbf{Observed ancestry sorting far exceeds a neutral demographic null, even though the
same simulation reproduces the empirical local-LD landscape.} Per-SNP sorting was estimated on
each of 1000 coalescent-scale DIEM replicates exactly as in Figure~2 and compared to the
empirical observation (orange diamonds). \textbf{(a)} Percentage of diagnostic SNPs called
sorted at each threshold $\tau=0.5$--$0.8$: the simulated null (green) sits at essentially zero
across every replicate, whereas the observed values are $22.0/13.6/9.0/4.7\,\%$.
\textbf{(b)} The same contrast as raw sorted-SNP counts ($+1$, log scale): at $\tau=0.5$ the
observed count is $\sim$11{,}400 sorted SNPs while the \emph{maximum} over all 1000 null
replicates is 3 (only 194/1000 replicates produce even one sorted SNP), and at $\tau=0.8$ no
replicate produces a single sorted SNP. The separation is complete at every threshold
(empirical $p=0.001$, the 1000-replicate floor; $z\!\ge\!2\times10^{4}$). Observed sorting is
also directionally biased ($70\,\%$ toward \Faq{} at $\tau=0.5$); the null has essentially no
sorted loci and therefore no testable direction.}
{% methods
\emph{Simulated null.} 1000 replicate data sets simulated under the study's neutral
demographic model for the hybrid zone, run through the identical DIEM pipeline and read from
\texttt{data/diem\_outs\_demo/diem\_boot$\langle$N$\rangle$\_output.bed}. Each replicate is a
\emph{matched} design --- the same 20 hybrid populations at near-identical sizes and 15\,+\,15
parental references --- so sorting is estimated per population exactly as in the observed data.

\emph{Sorting estimator.} For every replicate we ran the Figure~2 estimator unchanged
(\texttt{ohta\_fast\_prepare}\,$\rightarrow$\,\texttt{parallelism\_stats}, near-fixation floor
$\varphi=0.85$, \texttt{binom} direction rule, $\alpha=0.05$), orienting each replicate to the
\Faq{} allele by \emph{its own} parental allele-frequency difference. DIEM's three states enter
directly as $0/1/2$ dosage; because orientation is by parents the DIEM polarity is irrelevant.
We verified the simulated parents are differentiated (median parental allele-frequency
difference $0.77$), so the null's absence of sorting is genuine, not an orientation artefact.
At each $\tau\in\{0.5,0.6,0.7,0.8\}$ we tallied the percentage and count of sorted SNPs and the
\Faq{}-directional fraction, and compared the observation to the 1000-replicate null
($z$-score and empirical one-sided $p=(1+\#\{\text{null}\ge\text{obs}\})/1001$). Estimated at
the SNP level only. Generated by \texttt{module\_di25/R/di25\_sorting\_emp\_vs\_sim.R}
(resumable, per-replicate cache).}
{% note
\emph{The same null reproduces LD but not sorting --- so sorting, not LD, is the non-neutral
signal.} This demographic simulation matches the empirical per-window local-LD landscape closely
(Figures~7--8: windowed-median $\mathrm{ld_w}$, per-window Pearson $0.91$, the OLS fit on the 1:1
line). Yet it produces essentially no parallel
ancestry sorting. Local LD is therefore largely a product of the shared recombination landscape
and neutral demography, whereas parallel near-fixation toward the \emph{same} parental ancestry
across many independent populations is not generated by drift/admixture at all --- it is the
signature the observed data carry in excess of this null, and it is \Faq{}-biased. This
strengthens the earlier neutral-null contrast (\emph{F}\textsubscript{ST}/sorting far exceeds
drift while LD does not) using a null that now \emph{matches} empirical LD, removing LD-level as an
alternative explanation.

\emph{Scope and caveats.} Per-SNP only here; the per-LD-cluster (eMLG) null is not yet run
(it requires re-clustering each replicate, or applying the empirical cluster definitions as a
same-units shortcut). The null is a single demographic model, so it bounds what neutral
demography of this design can produce rather than exhausting all neutral scenarios. Preliminary
pending the reseeded-DI rerun (see Fig.~2).}

\figblock{fig_bdmi_recomb.pdf}
{Figure 10 --- BDMI candidacy has the opposite recombination architecture to sorting}
{% caption
\textbf{Unlike ancestry sorting, BDMI candidacy does not concentrate at low recombination ---
if anything it favours high recombination --- so the BDMI\,$\leftrightarrow$\,sorting overlap
(Figs.~4--6) is not a shared low-recombination artefact.} Analysis of the same DI\,$>-25$
diagnostic SNPs ($n=51{,}612$) used in Figures~2--6, each assigned a map-interpolated
recombination rate. \textbf{(a)} Fraction of diagnostic SNPs falling inside the BDMI candidate
regions across recombination deciles (\emph{x}: median cM/Mb per decile), at a stringent
($\chi^2=0.05$, dark) and a permissive ($\chi^2=0.01$, light) cutoff; Wilson 95\,\% intervals.
At the permissive cutoff the BDMI-candidate fraction climbs steeply with recombination (from
$\sim$12\,\% in the lowest decile to $\sim$85\,\% in the high-recombination deciles); at the
stringent cutoff it is low and flat ($\sim$2--14\,\%). \textbf{(b)} Recombination coefficient of
BDMI candidacy --- the slope of a logistic regression of $P(\text{SNP inside a BDMI region})$ on
$\log_{10}$ recombination, fitted across all diagnostic SNPs --- at each $\chi^2$ cutoff, with a
chromosome block-bootstrap 95\,\% interval. It is strongly \emph{positive} at permissive cutoffs
($+2.3$ at $\chi^2=0.005$, interval excluding zero: candidacy concentrated at \emph{high}
recombination) and fades to $\sim$0 at the stringent cutoffs. The grey band is the same
coefficient for ancestry sorting itself ($P(\text{sorted at }\tau=0.6)$, fitted on the identical
SNP set): $-0.5$ (95\,\% CI $[-1.1,\,0.0]$), \emph{negative} --- sorting concentrated at low
recombination. BDMI candidacy and sorting therefore point in opposite directions on the
recombination axis, or (at stringent cutoffs) are decoupled from it.}
{% methods
\emph{Data.} The 51{,}612 diagnostic SNPs of Figure~2, each given a recombination rate (cM/Mb)
by per-chromosome linear interpolation of the linkage map
(\texttt{data/Frufa\_DTOL\_PR.ref\_genome.recmap}), and flagged inside/outside the merged BDMI
candidate regions at each of the 23 $\chi^2$ cutoffs.

\emph{Panel a.} Recombination deciles were defined on the diagnostic-SNP distribution; within
each decile we plot the fraction of SNPs inside the BDMI regions with Wilson 95\,\% intervals, at
$\chi^2=0.05$ and $\chi^2=0.01$.

\emph{Panel b.} For each cutoff we fitted a logistic regression of the binary ``inside a BDMI
region'' indicator on $\log_{10}(\text{recombination}+0.1)$ across all diagnostic SNPs; 95\,\%
intervals are from a genomic block bootstrap resampling whole chromosomes (1000 replicates). The
reference band is the identical fit with the response replaced by ``ancestry-sorted at
$\tau=0.6$'' ($\varphi=0.85$, binomial direction test), computed on the same SNP set and bootstrap.
Generated by \texttt{module\_di25/Figures.R} (\texttt{fig\_bdmi\_recomb}).}
{% note
\emph{Why this matters for Figures~4--6.} A natural worry is that BDMI regions overlap sorted loci
simply because both crowd into the same low-recombination genome. They do not: sorting concentrates
at low recombination (Figs.~2--3; grey band here), whereas BDMI candidacy is recombination-neutral
at the stringent cutoffs where the overlap signal lives, and positively recombination-biased at
permissive cutoffs. The overlap therefore occurs \emph{despite} opposing recombination tendencies,
which argues against a shared-architecture explanation.

\emph{Caveat on the positive trend.} The rise of permissive-cutoff candidacy with recombination may
be partly a property of the DMI screen rather than biology: two-locus distortion is easier to read
between loci that recombine freely, so a between-locus test can recover more candidates in
high-recombination regions. Either way the key comparison --- BDMI candidacy \emph{not} tracking
sorting's low-recombination concentration --- holds. The sorting reference is fitted at the SNP
level for a like-for-like comparison and is accordingly weaker than the LD-reduced estimate in
Figure~2 (SNPs over-weight the large blocks and are non-independent). Preliminary pending the
reseeded-DI rerun (see Fig.~2).}
