# Supplementary Methods

## Rationale

Repeated ancestry sorting across the hybrid populations underlies our interpretation of parallel, selection-driven evolution. A potential concern is that the hybrid populations may not be independent: if they descended from a shared admixed source or were connected by ongoing gene flow, the same loci could appear "sorted" across populations without independent selection in each, which would inflate the apparent parallelism. We addressed this in two complementary ways. First, we asked whether the observed sorting exceeds what a neutral demographic model of the hybrid zone produces, using simulations calibrated to reproduce the empirical linkage-disequilibrium (LD) structure so that they constitute a faithful neutral reference. Second, and most directly, we measured the genetic differentiation among the empirical populations themselves, reasoning that non-independent populations sharing recent ancestry would be weakly differentiated, whereas strong differentiation would show that the populations are effectively independent and that shared ancestry cannot account for the repeated sorting.

## Neutral demographic simulations

We generated 1,000 replicate neutral datasets under a coalescent model of the hybrid zone, matched to the empirical design of 20 hybrid populations with the same sample sizes, and processed each through the identical DIEM pipeline used for the empirical data. Every statistic described below was computed in exactly the same way on the empirical data and on each replicate, so that the observed values could be compared directly to the resulting null distributions.

## Local linkage disequilibrium (to validate the null)

To establish that the simulations are a faithful neutral model of the genome — a prerequisite for using them as a null for sorting and differentiation — we compared the local LD landscape. For each marker we computed ld~w~, the median composite *r*^2^ among neighbouring markers out to the distance at which the fitted chromosome-wise LD-decay curve reaches ρ = 0.95 (hybrids only), and summarised it as the median in 500-kb windows along the genome. A close match between empirical and simulated ld~w~ indicates that the simulation captures the recombination-driven LD structure and therefore provides a well-calibrated neutral reference; the simulated envelope is the 2.5–97.5% range across replicates.

## Ancestry sorting (to quantify parallel fixation against the null)

To quantify parallel ancestry fixation and test it against the null, we scored each diagnostic SNP for sorting. Within each population a marker was considered near-fixed when its parent-oriented allele frequency reached φ = 0.85 toward *F. aquilonia*, or fell to ≤ 1 − φ toward *F. polyctena*. A marker was called sorted when the proportion of the 20 populations near-fixed for the same ancestry reached a threshold τ (evaluated at 0.5, 0.6, 0.7 and 0.8), and its direction was assigned by a two-sided binomial test of the counts near-fixed toward each species (α = 0.05). Applying the identical procedure to each simulated replicate yields the neutral distribution of the number of sorted SNPs; empirical significance is one-sided, *P* = (1 + #{replicates ≥ observed}) / (1 + 1000).

## Among-population differentiation by diagnostic index (the direct test of independence)

To measure differentiation among the populations directly, and to separate it from ancestry-informativeness, we computed Weir & Cockerham (1984) *F*~ST~ among the 20 hybrid populations at genome-wide LD-reduced markers (the best SNP per LD cluster, so that *F*~ST~ is not inflated by linked markers) and stratified the markers by DiagnosticIndex (DI). We used fixed DI intervals rather than quantiles, so that the small number of near-neutral background markers (DI ≤ −90) are resolved rather than averaged in with the far more numerous ancestry-informative markers. This design lets differentiation be read as a function of ancestry-informativeness: near-neutral background loci report the baseline differentiation of the populations, and hence the implied gene flow (*Nm*), while diagnostic loci report the differentiation of ancestry itself. The simulations contain only the diagnostic markers, so they anchor the high-DI end of this axis. Because low pooled-parental minor-allele frequency (MAF) — which is possible only at low-DI loci — can mechanically deflate *F*~ST~, we additionally recomputed the empirical curve within four strata of pooled-parental MAF, to test whether the low-DI decline is an MAF artefact. As an additional structure-sensitive comparison we computed the between-chromosome ("background") LD, the 95th percentile of inter-chromosomal *r*^2^, for the empirical data and each replicate.

# Supplementary Results

## The simulation reproduces the empirical LD landscape

The simulations closely reproduce the empirical LD landscape (Supplementary Fig. S1). The genome-wide mean windowed-median ld~w~ is 0.265 in the empirical data versus 0.260 in the simulations, the two agree window-by-window with a Pearson correlation of 0.91, and the characteristic high-LD blocks and low-LD chromosome arms align. The simulations therefore provide a well-calibrated neutral reference against which the observed ancestry patterns can be judged.

## Ancestry sorting greatly exceeds the neutral null

Against this LD-matched null, ancestry sorting in the empirical data is far in excess of neutral expectations. In the empirical data, 22.0%, 13.6%, 9.0% and 4.7% of diagnostic SNPs were sorted at τ = 0.5, 0.6, 0.7 and 0.8, respectively. The neutral simulations produced essentially no sorting: across all 1,000 replicates the largest number of sorted SNPs at τ = 0.5 was three, with any sorted SNP at all in only 194 of the 1,000 replicates, and none at τ ≥ 0.8. The observed values therefore lie far outside the null distribution at every threshold (empirical *P* = 0.001, the resolution limit of 1,000 replicates; Supplementary Fig. S2), and the sorting is directional, biased toward *F. aquilonia* (70% of the directionally resolved SNPs at τ = 0.5).

## Differentiation scales with ancestry-informativeness and exceeds the null

The empirical populations are strongly differentiated, and this differentiation scales with ancestry-informativeness. Empirical *F*~ST~ rises from 0.049 at near-neutral background markers (DI ≤ −90) to a plateau of approximately 0.28 at diagnostic markers (Supplementary Fig. S3). At the same diagnostic markers, among-population *F*~ST~ in the neutral simulations is only about 0.021, so empirical differentiation at ancestry-informative loci exceeds the neutral expectation roughly 13-fold. This dose-response is not an artefact of parental allele frequency: within each of the four pooled-parental-MAF strata the empirical curve reproduces both the diagnostic plateau and the low-DI drop, and it does so most clearly in the highest-MAF stratum ([0.35, 0.5]), where the DI ≤ −90 bin falls to *F*~ST~ = 0.03; conversely the low-MAF stratum ([0, 0.1]) sits at ~0.28–0.31 wherever it occurs, showing that low parental MAF does not deflate *F*~ST~ here (Supplementary Fig. S3). The between-chromosome (background) LD is likewise higher in the empirical data (0.067) than in the simulations (0.024).

## High differentiation rules out non-independence of the hybrid populations

Together these results resolve the non-independence concern. If the hybrid populations shared recent common ancestry — the scenario under which repeated sorting could arise without independent selection — they would be genetically similar and therefore weakly differentiated. Instead they are strongly differentiated at ancestry-informative loci (*F*~ST~ ≈ 0.28), which is incompatible with their being non-independent replicates of a common source. At the same time, the low *F*~ST~ at neutral-background markers (0.049, corresponding to *Nm* ≈ 4.8) shows that the populations do exchange genes; the strong differentiation is thus specific to ancestry-informative loci and is maintained against gene flow, rather than being a passive consequence of shared history. Because the neutral demographic simulation reproduces the empirical LD structure yet generates neither the sorting nor the ancestry differentiation, both signals are non-neutral properties of the data that cannot be attributed to the demographic and recombinational structure of the genome, nor to non-independence among the hybrid populations. The repeated ancestry sorting is therefore best interpreted as parallel, selection-driven fixation across genuinely independent, strongly differentiated populations.
