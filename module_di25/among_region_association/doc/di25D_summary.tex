\noindent\textit{\small The intrinsic-side, pair-level test of the headline question, restricted to the
high-DI species-diagnostic markers (DI $>-25$) and their from-scratch DI25 LD clustering. Between
\emph{unlinked} LD-reduced units, is there association beyond what shared ancestry and relatedness
predict --- the signature of a two-locus Dobzhansky--Muller incompatibility (DMI)? The answer,
descriptive and pre-simulation, is largely negative for \emph{trans} (different-chromosome) DMIs; the
only structure-corrected leads are long-range \emph{within}-chromosome associations. This mirrors the
canonical Module~D result on the high-DI subset.}

\section{Materials and Methods}

\paragraph{Units and gate.}
Units are the from-scratch DI25 LD clusters (\texttt{di25\_clustering\_cM5}; 5\,cM cap), folded into a
single aqu-oriented dosage matrix of $165$ hybrids $\times$ $11{,}052$ units --- the eMLG consensus for
clusters with $>2$ markers and the representative SNP for $1$--$2$-marker clusters. Orientation to \Faq{}
was verified against the parent-based pooled aquilonia frequency ($r=0.99$). Each unit is annotated with
the DI25 sorting gate (\texttt{di25\_sorting\_emlg}: \texttt{differentiated}, DI, \texttt{sort\_class},
\texttt{prop\_fixed}, \texttt{uni\_score}, \texttt{bi\_score}) and a per-unit \emph{hybrid} metric table
(within-hybrid MAF, mean within-population MAF, observed/expected heterozygosity, per-locus $F_{ST}$/$F_{IS}$,
number of segregating populations). ``Differentiated'' is near-vacuous here ($11{,}042/11{,}052$), as every
DI25 marker is diagnostic. \emph{Unlinked} means different chromosome, or the same chromosome $>\text{LINK\_CM}=10$\,cM
apart on the genetic map.

\paragraph{Ohta among-population arm (descriptive / null instrument).}
For unlinked pairs we compute the Ohta (1982) two-locus decomposition (\Dis, \Dst, \Dpst, $D'^2_{\mathrm{is}}$)
and $\Rst = \operatorname{cor}$ of the $20$ per-population mean dosages. This arm is retained as a descriptive
distribution-generator and as Module~E's measurement instrument, not as a discovery screen.

\paragraph{EMMAX pairwise-candidate network.}
Rather than test a few foci against all $\sim$11k units, we define a marginal candidate set and test only
the pairwise associations \emph{among} the candidates --- a symmetric, network-native scan. The candidate set
is the \textbf{bidirectional} units, $\texttt{bi\_score}\ge0.2$ ($K=2{,}083$): loci that sort strongly but in
\emph{inconsistent} directions across the replicate populations (the equal-fitness / DMI-plausible signature).
Because a bidirectional locus must segregate to sort both ways, this criterion also enforces appreciable
minor-allele frequency (minimum within-hybrid $\mathrm{MAF}=0.16$). All $\sim$$2.17\times10^{6}$ unlinked pairs
among the candidates are tested. Each candidate dosage is treated as a quantitative trait and regressed on each
partner in an EMMAX linear mixed model whose random effect $K$ is a \emph{double}-LOCO VanRaden genomic-relationship
matrix (excluding both the focal and partner chromosome), built --- like the top $N_{\mathrm{PC}}=10$ genome
principal components used as fixed covariates --- from \emph{all} differentiated units, so each test carries the
full ancestry-axis and relatedness correction; only the tested traits/partners shrink to the candidate set. The
$cis/trans$ sign is the sign of the GLS coefficient. Alongside the structure-corrected EMMAX $r^2$ we record three
cheap uncorrected estimates per pair: the plain pairwise $r^2$ ($r^2_{\mathrm{raw}}$), the among-population
correlation \Rst, and the median within-population correlation \wpr.

\paragraph{Significance and read-out.}
Significance is a pooled (whole-experiment) Benjamini--Hochberg FDR over all unlinked pairs, mapped to a single
$r^2$ threshold through the EMMAX statistic $F=r^2/(1-r^2)\cdot(n-2)$. We report the genomic-inflation factor
$\lambda$ but do \emph{not} genomic-control the FDR: it is a pre-filter, and the discriminant is Module~E's
recombination-matched neutral null. The honest read-out then applies, in order: a within-chromosome
average-linkage merge; a \textbf{within-population} leave-one-population-out leverage filter (keep an edge only if
$\min_k |\mathrm{median}_{\text{pops}\neq k}\,\wpr| \ge 0.3$); a low-recombination annotation (carried to Module~E,
never a filter); and cross-chromosome modules. \emph{The leverage is within-population, not among-population}
(\Rst): the decomposition (below) shows the EMMAX signal is the within-population one, so an among-population
leverage would select \emph{for} structure and discard the DMI candidates.

\paragraph{Software.}
Run from the repository root. Ohta functions and \texttt{consensus\_dosage} are provided by \texttt{LDscnR}
(\texttt{devtools::load\_all}); the EMMAX engine (Li, Kemppainen, Rastas \& Meril\"a) is a local copy. Figures use
\texttt{circlize}, \texttt{GGally}, and base graphics.

\section{Results}

\paragraph{The among-population arm is drift/structure, not systematic epistasis.}
On the high-DI data the Ohta decomposition reproduces the canonical Module~D signature: the among-population variance
component dominates the within-population one ($\Dst\gg\Dis$, fraction $1.00$) while the \emph{systematic}
among-population LD is essentially zero ($\Dpst\approx0$). This is Ohta's classic drift signature (large variance,
no systematic covariance). Importantly, $\Rst$ is the exact counterpart of $\Dpst$, not of $\Dst$: measured directly,
$\operatorname{cor}(\Rst^2,\Dpst)=0.74$ versus $\operatorname{cor}(\Rst^2,\Dst)=0.02$. The epistasis-relevant
(systematic) axis is therefore near-empty before any null.

\paragraph{The two LD axes are a Wahlund decomposition (Fig.~\ref{fig:pairs}).}
Total (raw) pairwise LD splits into a within-population term and an among-population covariance-of-frequencies term.
In these data the among-population term dominates raw LD, and the EMMAX correction isolates the within-population
term --- an almost perfect double-dissociation: on $|r|$, raw LD tracks $|\Rst|$ ($0.70$) but not within-population
$|\wpr|$ ($0.05$), whereas EMMAX tracks $|\wpr|$ ($0.41$) but not $|\Rst|$ ($0.07$). This is precisely why EMMAX is the
right instrument for the intrinsic (within-deme) DMI question, and why the leverage must be within-population.

\paragraph{Candidate selection and a low-MAF lesson.}
An earlier candidate gate ($\texttt{prop\_fixed}\in[0.5,0.8)$) produced $\sim$4 leads that were all low-MAF
($0.05$--$0.13$), near-fixed in one direction, with within-population LD carried by a thin heterozygote layer ---
a low-MAF inflation artifact ($\operatorname{cor}(\text{min-MAF},|\wpr|)=-0.33$; $74\%$ of edges had min-MAF $<0.15$)
that the within-population leverage does not catch. MAF is not a selective filter on DI25 (median within-hybrid MAF
$0.38$, so a MAF-only floor keeps $\sim$10k units and yields an all-vs-all of $\sim$$5\times10^7$ pairs). Bidirectionality
resolves this: it is DMI-motivated and simultaneously enforces $\mathrm{MAF}\ge0.16$.

\paragraph{The network is near-empty (Fig.~\ref{fig:circos},~\ref{fig:snp}).}
On the bidirectional set the genomic inflation is $\lambda=1.28$. This is not a broken correction (the full focal-vs-all
set gives $\lambda\approx1.02$) but the candidate \emph{selection}: strongly-sorted loci share diffuse residual
co-ancestry that lifts the whole null, with no fat tail. At $q<0.01$ ($r^2_{\mathrm{crit}}=0.245$) exactly \textbf{one}
pair clears the pooled FDR and survives the within-population leverage: \textbf{F9614--F9879} (both Chr\,7, $>10$\,cM
apart), coupling, with within-population $\wpr=0.68$ robust across $11$ informative populations (LOO $0.59$), appreciable
MAF $0.27/0.45$, and $\Rst=0.50$; its $r^2=0.245$ sits at the stringent threshold, so it is the single most-significant,
borderline pair. At the looser $q<0.05$ ($r^2_{\mathrm{crit}}=0.159$) seven edges are significant and five survive
leverage, all appreciable-MAF ($0.27$--$0.48$) and robust across $11$--$13$ populations.

\paragraph{Every lead is same-chromosome; no robust \emph{trans}-DMI (Fig.~\ref{fig:detect}b).}
Six of the seven $q<0.05$ FDR edges are same-chromosome ($11$--$92$\,cM apart); the single different-chromosome (true
\emph{trans}) edge fails the leverage filter. The reason is a difference in linkage scale between the two axes:
among-population LD ($\Rst^2$) decays to the inter-chromosomal baseline by $\sim$10\,cM (the admixture-LD scale that
justifies the unlinked rule, validated by the Ohta arm), but within-population LD keeps declining well past $10$\,cM,
and the leads sit far above the average as long-range within-chromosome outliers. The $10$\,cM ``unlinked'' rule is thus
valid for the among-population axis but does \emph{not} make a same-chromosome pair \emph{trans} on the within-population
axis. The surviving leads are therefore long-range within-chromosome LD (structural / intra-chromosomal coadaptation),
not trans-DMIs, and the genuine trans signal is empty.

\paragraph{The among-population candidates (Fig.~\ref{fig:rst}).}
Complementary to the within-population leads are the pairs with the strongest \emph{among}-population correlation.
The top $|\Rst|$ different-chromosome pairs ($|\Rst|=0.86$--$0.91$) show strong parallel (coupling) or anti-parallel
(repulsion) co-sorting of the two loci across the $20$ replicate populations --- the population blocks track or
anti-track --- yet these same pairs have within-population $\wpr\approx0$ and EMMAX $r^2\approx0$: within demes the two
loci are independent, and the structure correction strips them entirely. They are the systematic among-population axis
(\Dpst), i.e.\ the equal-fitness-DMI candidates that the detectability argument places in the confounded blind spot: the
population-level co-sorting is genuine but indistinguishable from shared-founding co-differentiation (drift) without a
recombination-matched neutral null. We report them as candidates only.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Largely negative for trans-DMIs on the high-DI data.}
After a structure-corrected pairwise scan, a stringent pooled FDR, and a within-population robustness filter, the
DI25 among-region signal reduces to a handful of appreciable-MAF, multi-population associations that are \emph{all
same-chromosome}, plus an empty different-chromosome (trans) axis. This is the high-DI analogue of Module~D's
``largely negative, small residue'' conclusion, and it is consistent with the Ohta arm's $\Dpst\approx0$: neither the
within-population axis (EMMAX) nor the systematic among-population axis (Ohta) carries a widespread pairwise
incompatibility signal.

\paragraph{Method lessons.}
Three points generalise. (i) Raw two-locus LD in a structured, admixed sample is dominated by among-population
co-differentiation (a Wahlund term); only the structure-corrected (EMMAX/within-population) axis speaks to intrinsic
incompatibilities, and the robustness (leverage) filter must be applied on \emph{that} axis. (ii) On a diagnostic-marker
panel, MAF is not a selective filter, and gates that select strongly-sorted loci (e.g.\ \texttt{prop\_fixed}) preferentially
pick near-fixed, low-MAF loci whose pairwise LD is inflated; a bidirectionality gate is both DMI-motivated and MAF-enforcing.
(iii) The admixture-LD ``unlinked'' distance ($\sim$10\,cM), validated for the \emph{among}-population axis, is too short for
the \emph{within}-population axis; a clean trans-DMI screen should restrict to different-chromosome pairs.

\paragraph{Detectability window and the strength of the negative (Fig.~\ref{fig:detect}a, and the schematic).}
An equal-fitness DMI builds within-population LD while both loci still segregate --- which EMMAX detects --- but once both
loci fix within every deme the within-population variation (and thus the detectable LD) vanishes, leaving only the
among-population co-sorting direction, which is confounded by the shared founding LD common to all $20$ replicates. We are
therefore powered for \emph{in-progress} DMIs and blind to \emph{completed} ones. Crucially, the blind spot is currently
small: only $\sim$1\% of loci are near-fixed, sorting is mostly incomplete, and every candidate and lead sits in the
detectable window. The near-empty result therefore reads as ``no detectable \emph{in-progress} pairwise DMIs'' and is not
primarily a blind-spot artifact --- although completed DMIs remain the principled blind spot.

\paragraph{Outlook.}
The one Module-E-bound lead, F9614--F9879, is a within-Chr\,7 pair; its LD, and that of the same-chromosome leads at
$q<0.05$, must be judged against a recombination-matched neutral null (Module~E) that models the shared founding history
--- especially for the low-recombination, structurally-plausible pairs. A different-chromosome-only re-run would state the
\emph{trans}-DMI negative cleanly.
