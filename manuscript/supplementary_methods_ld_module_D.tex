\section{Scope and analytical principles}\label{sec:scope}

These methods describe a screen for pair-specific, two-locus incompatibilities between
\emph{unlinked} LD-reduced units, complementary to the per-locus ancestry-sorting analyses
(which measure the \emph{direction} and extent of sorting) and to the climate analyses
(which test the extrinsic axis). The same LD-reduced units, expected multi-locus genotype
(eMLG) consensus dosages, and parental gate are reused; the methods for their construction
are given in the companion document.

An intrinsic incompatibility can leave three traces in replicate hybrids, and separating
them organises the analysis. (1)~\emph{Unidirectional} sorting, in which one parental
combination is fitter, drives the same ancestry toward fixation repeatedly; this is the
per-locus signal of the sorting analyses and is, by construction, invisible to any
two-locus test, because linkage disequilibrium is bounded by the allele frequencies and
vanishes as either locus approaches fixation. (2)~\emph{Bidirectional} sorting, expected
under an equal-fitness incompatibility, fixes opposite compatible combinations in different
replicates; its single-locus parallelism test is null by construction, and its signal lives
at the pair level. (3)~\emph{Coupling maintained within populations} is elevated two-locus
LD inside demes, which is badly confounded by cryptic relatedness among nestmates. A
two-locus screen must therefore condition on both the genome-wide ancestry gradient and the
colony structure before any residual association can be read as pair-specific.

Three principles guided the analysis, consistent with the companion methods. First, the
LD-reduced units are treated as complexity-reduced analytical units, not as strictly
independent tests; residual dependence is controlled or annotated where it matters. Second,
a variable used as a predictor was not also used to select the analysed data. Third, the
twenty hybrid populations are not assumed to be fully independent replicates; analyses using
among-population variation either condition on it or are interpreted descriptively, and the
final intrinsic call is deferred to a neutral simulation.

\section{Units, orientation, and the unlinked definition}\label{sec:units}

The analytical unit is the eMLG consensus dosage of an LD cluster (continuous, on an
\Faq-oriented $0$--$2$ scale; no hard-calling), restricted to the parent-differentiated
units used as informative markers throughout. Consensus orientation and the parental gate
(pooled parental MAF $\geq0.15$) follow the companion methods, so that a unit's dosage and
its diagnostic index (DI) are on consistent scales.

Two units were treated as \emph{unlinked} when they lay on different chromosomes, or on the
same chromosome more than $\mathrm{LINK}_{\mathrm{cM}}=10$~cM apart on the genetic map.
Distance was taken on the map rather than in base pairs because admixture LD decays with the
recombination fraction, not with physical distance: at the recombination rates of this
genome, $10$~cM corresponds to $\sim$99\% decay of admixture LD over the $\sim$50
generations since contact. Per-unit map positions were the median member-marker position
interpolated from the recombination map. This is the same threshold used to define the
LD-reduced units, so ``unlinked'' pairs are those the clustering already treats as distinct
regions.

\section{Structure-corrected two-locus association (EMMAX)}\label{sec:emmax}

\subsection{Rationale and model}

To ask whether two units are associated \emph{beyond} what shared ancestry and relatedness
predict, we treat one unit's consensus dosage as a quantitative trait and test its
association against every other differentiated unit in a linear mixed model,
\begin{equation}
  y_i = \mu + \beta\,x_j + u + \varepsilon,\qquad
  u\sim N(0,\sigma_g^2 K),\quad \varepsilon\sim N(0,\sigma_e^2 I),
  \label{eq:emmax}
\end{equation}
where $y_i$ is the focal unit's dosage across the 164 individuals, $x_j$ a candidate
partner, and $K$ a genomic-relationship matrix (EMMAX; Li, Kemppainen, Rastas \& Meril\"a).
This is a genome scan in which the ``phenotype'' is one unit and the ``markers'' are all the
others; variance components are estimated once under the null and reused for every partner.

\subsection{Why one random effect handles both confounds}

The leading eigenvectors of $K$ are the ancestry/structure principal components, so
conditioning on $K$ removes the genome-wide ancestry gradient at the individual level; and
because $K$ also encodes sibling relatedness among nestmates, the Wahlund inflation of
within-population LD is regressed out in the \emph{same} term. Three choices are load-bearing:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Basis of $K$.} $K$ was built from the ancestry-\emph{informative}
        (differentiated) units, not a neutral, LD-pruned background. The confound to be
        absorbed is the \emph{selected} ancestry-sorting axis carried by those units; a
        neutral basis dilutes that eigenvector and under-corrects, inflating the genomic
        control factor to $\lambda\sim1.2$ and leaking the ancestry gradient back as spurious
        positive associations.
  \item \textbf{Double leave-one-chromosome-out.} Because the scan is symmetric, both the
        focal and the partner chromosome are dropped from $K$, so neither tested unit's own
        region deflates its association. This keeps the test calibrated ($\lambda\approx
        0.98$--$1.05$; Fig.~\ref{figD:emmax}).
  \item \textbf{Genome-PC conditioning.} Each focal trait was residualised on the top ten
        genome-wide principal components before the mixed model, because $K$ cannot remove a
        \emph{secondary} structure axis concentrated on the very chromosomes double-LOCO
        drops. After this step the random-focal control returns essentially no significant
        partner, while calibration holds.
\end{enumerate}
Each association is signed by its structure-corrected generalised-least-squares coefficient
(coupling, $+$; repulsion, $-$), recovering both cis and trans hits. A first, targeted run
scanned 51 focal units (bidirectional candidates, extreme among-population-covariance units,
and controls including a random-focal set for calibration).

\todo{An unbiased genome-wide focal scan (or a larger random focal sample) is required to
report an honest genome-wide hit rate rather than the enrichment of a targeted focal set; a
full all-versus-all scan ($\sim10^{9}$ tests) is unnecessary and the random control fixes the
scale. This is scheduled with the Module~E run.}

\section{Cross-chromosome paralogy, and why distance cannot remove it}\label{sec:paralogy}

The genetic-distance rule cannot catch cross-chromosome duplicates: two units on different
chromosomes are at infinite recombinational distance yet can be genotype-near-identical when
the same reads map to two assembly locations. These pairs are the loudest inter-chromosomal
``LD'' and are technical rather than epistatic. Both the among-population and the
structure-corrected read-outs therefore apply a signal-based filter.

The discriminant is the individual-level \emph{within-population} correlation of the two
units' consensus dosages, taken as the median over populations. Within-population correlation
removes the among-population (ancestry-axis) component, which is not what paralogy is about;
real long-range coupling in these data is moderate ($|r|\lesssim0.6$), whereas a duplication
sits at $|r|\approx1$ (a flipped duplicate gives $r\approx-1$, which $|r|$ still catches). A
pair was flagged as paralogous when the median within-population $|r|$ exceeded
$\mathrm{PARALOGY}_r=0.9$. Genotype concordance and per-unit excess observed heterozygosity
(a duplicated region inflates $H_o$) are stored alongside as corroboration (Fig.~\ref{figD:paralogy}).
The flag is treated as a paralogy \emph{candidate} rather than a proof, because an extremely
strong genuine within-deme incompatibility could also approach $|r|=1$; the grey zone
$0.6$--$0.9$ is retained as clean, conservatively.

\section{A single reproducible read-out of the scan}\label{sec:readout}

The scan was read out by one procedure rather than per-focal thresholds, so that the
structural and pair-specific components separate under a single criterion.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Global false-discovery control.} All unlinked (focal, partner) tests
        ($1.38\times10^{6}$) were pooled and controlled at a global Benjamini--Hochberg
        $q<Q_{\mathrm{FDR}}=0.01$ --- one whole-experiment error rate rather than 51
        independent per-focal corrections.
  \item \textbf{Paralogy filter} (Section~\ref{sec:paralogy}).
  \item \textbf{Third-level, context-only merge.} For this network alone (not the canonical
        clustering), same-chromosome units within $\mathrm{LINK}_{\mathrm{cM}}$ that remain
        correlated were collapsed into meta-nodes by \emph{average-linkage} clustering on
        $1-|r|$, cut at $|r|>\mathrm{MERGE}_r=0.5$, with pairs beyond
        $\mathrm{LINK}_{\mathrm{cM}}$ given an un-mergeable distance. Average linkage requires
        the \emph{whole} merged group to be mutually correlated; single linkage
        (connected components) chains uncorrelated ends and is not used. This prevents one
        low-recombination region from reading as many interacting loci.
  \item \textbf{Single-population leverage filter.} An edge was retained only if its
        among-population allele-frequency correlation survived leave-one-population-out
        ($\min_k|r_{-k}|\geq\mathrm{LEV}_{\mathrm{LOO}}=0.3$). Because a rare-allele edge that
        one deme drives fails this test, it subsumes any near-fixed / low-MAF cutoff, and no
        separate MAF filter is applied on the MAF-gated data.
\end{enumerate}

Cross-chromosome correlation \emph{modules} were then defined by average-linkage clustering
of the meta-node consensuses \emph{across} chromosomes, cut at $|r|>\mathrm{MODULE}_r=0.4$.
Unlike the within-chromosome merge, this groups co-varying regions genome-wide, so each
module is one coherent co-ancestry block used for display and for the single-module genotype
views.

\section{Recombination annotation, not a filter}\label{sec:recomb}

Each meta-node was annotated by its recombination percentile against the differentiated
background; units below the tenth percentile ($\mathrm{STRUCT}_{\mathrm{pct}}=0.10$;
centromeric and pericentromeric) were labelled \emph{structure}. This label is used only as
an annotation and \emph{never} as a filter, for a specific reason. Low recombination is not
evidence of neutrality: it is where coadapted and epistatic complexes are expected to
accumulate and persist, since recombination suppression is itself a hallmark of coadaptation.
For an unlinked pair the between-region recombination fraction is $0.5$ regardless of local
rate, so low local recombination does not ``hold'' cross-chromosome LD; it makes each region a
clean, low-noise ancestry block, i.e.\ the highest-signal marker of whatever residual
covariation remains after genome-PC conditioning --- finer neutral structure \emph{or}
selection. Every unit, low-recombination included, is therefore carried into the null of
Section~\ref{sec:null} and judged against a baseline matched to its own local recombination.

\section{Neutral, recombination-matched null}\label{sec:null}

Every filter above removes structure \emph{aligned} with what it conditions on; none can say
whether a residual pair, or a low-recombination module, exceeds what neutral,
recombination-matched drift would produce in twenty replicate populations of this size and
design. That is the role of the neutral simulation (Module~E), and two features are required.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Local-recombination matching.} Neutral admixture is simulated on the
        \emph{real} recombination map, with matched founder ancestries, generations, and
        sampling, and the identical scan and read-out (Sections~\ref{sec:emmax}--\ref{sec:readout})
        are applied to the simulated genotypes. A pair or module is a candidate only if its
        observed LD exceeds the neutral null \emph{at its own local recombination rate}, so a
        low-recombination pair is compared against a legitimately high baseline and can still
        surface.
  \item \textbf{Replicate-population parallelism.} Neutral relic co-ancestry drifts in random
        directions across the twenty populations, whereas epistatic selection maintains the
        same combination reproducibly; a module whose direction is parallel across many
        populations, or that exceeds the general low-recombination background, is corroborated
        as selection independently of the simulation.
\end{itemize}

\todo{Module E is not yet run. Decide whether local matching is achieved by simulating
directly on the real per-chromosome map (preferred; no binning artefacts) or by binning
observed pairs on local cM/Mb and comparing to rate-matched simulated pairs.}

\section{Statistical interpretation}\label{sec:interp}

The analyses are descriptive and conditional on the sampled populations, quality gates, and
analysis definitions. Structure correction removes covariation aligned with the ancestry axis
and colony relatedness; the recombination annotation flags where the neutral LD baseline is
high; and the genotype-level PCA characterises the structure module without proving its
neutrality (parallel or stabilising selection can also lower among-population differentiation).
No pair is called an incompatibility on the basis of the association scan alone. The
excess-over-neutral statements are made only against the recombination-matched null, and
individual candidates then require independent biological scrutiny.
