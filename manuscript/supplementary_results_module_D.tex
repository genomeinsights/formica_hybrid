\section{The scan is calibrated and its strongest signal is not pair-specific}\label{srD:scan}

Treating each differentiated LD-reduced unit's consensus dosage as a trait and testing it
against every other unit in a double-LOCO, genome-PC-conditioned mixed model gives a
calibrated scan (genomic control $\lambda\approx0.98$--$1.05$; Methods,
\emph{Structure-corrected two-locus association}). Read out as one procedure --- all
$1.38\times10^{6}$ unlinked tests pooled at global false-discovery rate $q<0.01$ (276 pairs),
then paralogy-filtered ($-29$ pairs), region-merged (156 to 127 meta-nodes), and
leverage-filtered ($-50$ edges) --- the scan yields \textbf{110 associations among 83
meta-nodes} (18 trans, 92 cis; Table~\ref{tabRD:cascade}; Fig.~\ref{figRD:network}).

The signal is dominated not by pair-specific incompatibility but by two removable or
structural components. First, the loudest cross-chromosome associations are \emph{technical}:
pairs whose median within-population $|r|$ approaches one are assembly paralogues, corroborated
by genotype concordance and excess heterozygosity (Methods, \emph{Cross-chromosome paralogy}).
Second, of the 110 surviving edges, \textbf{66 join two low-recombination ``structure''
meta-nodes} and only 13 join two candidate units (Table~\ref{tabRD:cascade}); the network is a
small number of high-degree hubs (four with degree $\geq10$) sitting in the lowest
recombination percentiles, rather than a field of isolated pairs (Fig.~\ref{figRD:network}).

\section{The structure module is individual-level admixture-block co-ancestry}\label{srD:structure}

The dominant object in the network is a single low-recombination complex whose members are
wired almost entirely to one another. A principal-component analysis over the 87 member loci
shows what it is \emph{not} (Fig.~\ref{figRD:structpca}). These loci separate individuals by
population \emph{less} than a random draw of differentiated loci: the mean per-locus
among-population $R^2$ is $0.43$, against $0.54$ for random differentiated loci and $0.64$ for
the candidate loci. They also load only weakly on the genome-wide principal components
($|r|\leq0.23$). Their cross-genome correlation is therefore neither among-population
geographic differentiation nor the dominant ancestry axis, but individual-level
admixture-block covariance: ancestral haplotype blocks co-inherited within individuals and,
because these regions recombine slowly, not yet broken up. This is why the hubs persist through
genome-PC conditioning, and it is read here directly from the genotypes rather than from the
recombination map.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.74\textwidth]{../Figures/moduleD_structure_pca.png}
\caption{\textbf{The structure module carries less among-population structure than baseline.}
PCA over the 87 loci of the structure module, individuals coloured by population. Real

\section{The co-ancestry resolves into several cross-chromosome modules}\label{srD:modules}

The structure ``module'' is not one block but several. Average-linkage clustering of the
meta-node consensuses across chromosomes (cut at $|r|>0.4$; Methods, \emph{A single
reproducible read-out}) resolves the network into 23 co-ancestry modules --- a dominant module
of 16 meta-nodes spanning 12 chromosomes and secondary modules of 14, 9 and 7 meta-nodes.
Plotted one module at a time, each is a single coherent block: across all of a module's SNPs on
six or more chromosomes an individual takes one of the three dosages \emph{together}, exactly as
a co-inherited admixture haplotype block should, and unrelated to the other modules that the
all-structure view mixes in (Fig.~\ref{figRD:modhm}). Drawing only the within-module edges on
the genome shows the same point: the co-ancestry resolves into a few disjoint, colour-coded
bundles (79 of the 110 edges) rather than one hairball (Fig.~\ref{figRD:modcirc}).

\section{The candidate residue}\label{srD:candidates}

Thirteen edges join two candidate meta-nodes (Table~\ref{tabRD:cand}; Fig.~\ref{figRD:candcirc}),
and they organise into three kinds. (i)~An aquilonia \emph{co-sorting} module anchored on
F33454 (Chr10; degree 12): its partners on Chr3/4/5/9/11/13/19 are almost all aquilonia-sorted,
i.e.\ coordinated ancestry co-selection toward the minor (aquilonia) background rather than an
obvious pairwise incompatibility --- this module belongs with the ancestry-sorting arms.
(ii)~A single strong, ancestry-\emph{independent} trans pair, F11431--F49480 (Chr3--Chr16;
$q=2.2\times10^{-10}$; both loci unsorted), the cleanest pairwise-incompatibility candidate the
network contains. (iii)~A few same-ancestry cis pairs (polyctena: F17114--F49429,
F15820--F26284; aquilonia: F22294--F39587), i.e.\ co-sorting, not repulsion. Stripped of
structure and of ancestry co-sorting, the pair-specific residue is F11431--F49480 together with
a couple of weak, leverage-stable trans pairs --- a handful of edges reached by one reproducible
criterion.

\section{Low recombination is a caveat, not a verdict}\label{srD:null}

The interpretation above is descriptive and stops short of an intrinsic call, for one reason.
Low recombination is not evidence of neutrality: it is where coadapted complexes are expected
to accumulate, so the ``structure'' annotation is used as context, never as a filter, and every
unit --- low-recombination included --- is carried into the neutral null. Because for an
unlinked pair the between-region recombination fraction is $0.5$ regardless of local rate, low
local recombination does not hold cross-chromosome LD; it makes each region a clean, low-noise
ancestry marker that lights up for whatever residual covariation remains after conditioning,
neutral or selected. The PCA above is consistent with neutral relic co-ancestry but does not
exclude selection, which parallel or stabilising forms can also render weakly differentiated
(Methods, \emph{Recombination annotation, not a filter}).

The clean discriminant is a neutral null matched to \emph{local} recombination (Methods,
\emph{Neutral, recombination-matched null}): every pair is judged against a baseline simulated
at its own recombination rate, so a low-recombination pair can still surface if its LD exceeds
that high baseline. Two corroborations are available before the simulation and both separate
selection from neutral relic co-ancestry: replicate-population parallelism (neutral drift takes
random directions across the twenty populations, whereas selection maintains the same
combination reproducibly) and specific exceedance of a module over the general
low-recombination background. The coherent multi-chromosome blocks (Fig.~\ref{figRD:modhm}) are
therefore the first coadaptation candidates to test, not the ones to set aside. Until the null
gives them a scale, the intrinsic screen reports a largely negative result --- the apparent
two-locus signal is structure, assembly, or single-deme leverage --- with a short, explicit list
of candidates to resolve.
